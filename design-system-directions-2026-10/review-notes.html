Choosing a clearer system for Clarity

The current interface uses bevels, glass, gradients, and shadows in many places. Four interactive directions test simpler ways to organize the same work. Choose the direction that makes finding, editing, and sharing a page easiest to understand.

What this decision covers

Compare the information hierarchy, control language, and pace of the library, page editor, share flow, and component states. Try each in light and dark mode and at narrow widths. V1 is a source-based reproduction of the current design for reference.

The prototypes use mock content and local interactions. They do not connect to customer data, recording, uploads, publishing, analytics, or the full editor. A polished prototype is evidence for a design choice, not proof that it is ready to ship.

Questions to use while comparing

  • Can you find the main action without scanning every control?
  • Does the page editor leave enough room for both preview and settings?
  • Do sharing, error, disabled, and focus states stay legible?

After a direction is selected

Integrate the system

Map the chosen tokens and component rules to the shared buttons, fields, navigation, menus, dialogs, and media controls.

Verify real work

Exercise the library, editors, sharing, dense forms, keyboard flow, touch targets, contrast, loading, and error paths in the running app.

Roll out in stages

Review the implementation in staging, resolve regressions, and release through the normal production process.

Review evidence

Fable 5.1 authored V2, V3, V4, and V5. Each direction then went through two independent Fable critique and revision cycles. The first critique informed revision 2; the second informed revision 3. All author and critic runs have verified model receipts. Review rounds and completion records.

Changes recorded across both revision cycles
DirectionChanges
V2Combined phone creation actions, removed duplicate sharing controls and card decorations, and repaired focus return from Share to its menu opener.
V3Reduced list columns and hid the idle inspector. Repaired checkbox styling, repeated Share handlers, and focus return after edits and section drawer actions.
V4Separated page sheet colors from the app theme, simplified Library and Share metadata, and repaired dialog focus when the link is off.
V5Removed duplicate inspector fields and actions, strengthened dark media edges, simplified phone creation, and preserved focus across selection and Share updates.

The final layout matrix contains 30 cases per direction, 120 total. It covers widths of 375, 390, 768, 1280, and 1440 pixels, the Library, Editor, and System views, and both light and dark themes. The recorded checks found no document overflow, failed resources, or unnamed controls in those cases.

The 375-pixel scan flagged controls below 44 pixels in all six cases per direction. Its results include small native inputs with larger labels and labelled specimens, so the flags need manual hit-area assessment. They do not establish that all touch targets pass. A separate recorded wrapper check confirmed that the latest theme and view survive rapid direction changes during loading.

Targeted browser checks also covered Share reopening and focus return, the V3 section drawer, V4 page-theme isolation, and V2 video source and aspect changes. The final independent review found three prototype behavior bugs; these were corrected before publication.

These are local mock interactions. The System views include labelled CSS state simulations. The checks do not certify every button or accessibility requirement, and they do not include a full screen-reader audit, real-device testing, or production workflow certification.
