Live transcript and title at Stop

Design for Clarity recordings. 2026-10-08. Covers what PR #1397 should do now. No code was written and nothing was deployed. Probes hit Workers AI on the Clarity account and never a prod Worker.

The answer. Transcribe the take while it is being recorded, in short HTTP chunks cut at pauses. Store the words on the server against the take, not against the Mux asset. Then everything that needs words (the speech bar, Auto trim, the title, and later captions) reads one set of words that is already on the server at Stop. The 60 s wait goes away because it was never transcription time. It was Mux making the asset ready, then Robots, then a 20 s poll.

Sub-second title is real when the rep stops talking before clicking Stop. In that case the server already titled the full transcript during the pause, and Stop only commits it, in about 0.2 to 0.3 s (inferred). When the rep talks right through the click, the title takes about 1.0 to 1.5 s at p50 and about 2 to 2.6 s at p90 (sums of measured hops). The title model sets that floor whatever the transport. A streaming socket would save about 0.3 to 0.5 s in that one case, but it costs 1.77x per minute and would be the codebase's first Durable Object. It isn't worth it yet.

#1397. Merge it now, unchanged, as the slow path and backstop. The fast path builds on it.

What you will see at Stop

You finish a sentence, pause, then click Stop. The finish screen opens with the speech bar and Auto trim already drawn. The header that says "Finish your video" today becomes an editable title field, and the title is already in it. Nothing loads and nothing changes after it appears.
You are still talking when you click. The speech bar and trim appear after about 0.3 to 0.9 s. The title field shows a quiet placeholder and the title fades in after about 1 to 1.5 s. If you start typing first, your text is the title and the automatic one never replaces it. Preview, Copy link and Open page never wait on any of this.

Titles are never shown before the server has committed them, so the finish screen, the share page and the editor always agree. That keeps WIL-3494's rule that the server owns the take.

Architecture

Browser (recorder)The existing 16 kHz tap feeds a pure reducer that cuts chunks. Cuts land at a 400 ms pause once 6 s have passed, at 20 s on the quietest 100 ms, and at pause, mic change and Stop. One chunk in flight. Unacked PCM sits in the IndexedDB journal next to the video timeslices. The leave guard counts unacked speech.
Chunk routePUT .../speech/chunks/:seq replaces /speech/window and keeps its checks (session, owner, recording, not discarded, inside the grant window). It runs nova-3 through the binding, then stores the words. A replayed seq with the same audio digest gets the stored answer back with no model call. Chunks that run past wall time since the grant are refused, which caps spend per take.
Words, one readerChunk rows (workspace, node, seq) hold [start, end, text] in take seconds, with pauses excluded. The finish intent writes a seal (chunk count, end sample, captured samples). cover() answers complete, gap, abstained or unsealed. readTakeWords is the only reader. It returns live words when the cover is complete and within 1 s of Mux's duration, and Robots words otherwise.
Title#1397's rules stand unchanged: title_source, a person always wins, and the share page follows the title. autoTitled gains a basis, either the asset (Robots path) or the live seal's chunk count. Drafts are made at pause cuts and stored on one per-take row. The finish intent commits a draft whose chunk count equals the seal's.
Robots becomes a fallback producer. It never reconciles with live words.
At asset ready, mux_speech is queued only when the live cover is not complete. That covers a gap, a non-English abstain, a browser that refused 16 kHz, a lost journal and every upload. Two stores with a fixed precedence and one reader is the same pattern settleAutoTrim already uses (recorder before Robots). Auto trim derives its edges from the live words, so /speech/recorder and recorder_speech_edges are deleted.

Lifecycle cases

CaseWhat happens
Pause and resumePause cuts a chunk. HTTP holds no connection, so there is no KeepAlive to manage. PCM time stays media time.
Network drops mid-takeChunks queue and retry with backoff. A 429 or 503 never ends the take. A backlog drains several chunks at once, about 0.25 to 0.6 s each (measured).
Tab closed, then recoveredThe journal holds unacked PCM. Recovery replays it and seals. Recovered takes get live words, which they cannot get today.
Retake or discardDiscard deletes the chunk rows. A late chunk gets a 404 from the existing discarded_at check.
Mic switchA switch cuts a chunk. Separately, a mic switch today silences both video audio and the tap, because nothing calls replaceTrack. That existing bug is a follow-up and is not fixed here. Until it is, the duration check sends those takes to Robots.
12 h takeAbout 2,200 chunk rows. One row per chunk stays under D1's row size limit. The title reads only head and tail lines, as #1397 already does.
Non-EnglishLanguage detection runs alongside English until a chunk has 3 detected words. On an abstain the client stops sending and Robots takes over.

Cost, limits, privacy

  • Cost. nova-3 over HTTP costs $0.0052 per audio minute (Cloudflare pricing page), about $0.026 for a 5 minute take. The WebSocket price is $0.0092. Title drafts are a few hundred input tokens each at $0.10 per million (inferred, well under a cent per take). Recordings with complete live words stop paying for Robots.
  • Limits. Workers AI allows 720 speech requests and 300 text requests per minute per account. At 3 to 6 chunks a minute per recorder that is about 120 to 240 concurrent recorders (inferred). Drafts are debounced to at most one per 15 s. The router's rate key moves from 10 per minute per owner to 60 per minute per video, so a backlog can drain.
  • Privacy. The whole take's audio now reaches Workers AI during recording, including takes the rep later discards. Today only head windows and the last 10 s do, and Robots hears every kept take. Audio stays in Cloudflare and is not stored. Cloudflare says it does not train on Workers AI content. Every call sets mip_opt_out. Audio never crosses the gateway, so the catalog's typed exception stays. Title calls cross the gateway with payload logging off, as in #1397. No transcript or title text reaches RUM, logs or Sentry. The privacy page needs one sentence saying spoken words are transcribed during recording to name and caption the video.

Alternatives considered

OptionWhy it lost
Streaming session: browser to a per-take Durable Object holding a nova-3 WebSocketIt wins only when the rep talks through the click, by 0.3 to 0.5 s, and ties at p90 because the title model dominates. It costs 1.77x per minute and would be the first Durable Object in the repo. It also needs a segment machine, KeepAlive through pauses, checkpoints for audio lost at connection start, and a batch repair path, and every deploy restarts live sessions. Streamed text matches Robots a little worse. The chunk transport sits behind one function, so a socket for only the open chunk can be added later if the census says talk-through is common.
AI Gateway realtime route for audioReturned 401. Whether it logs audio or honours payload-off on a socket is undocumented. It needs a proper API token probe before it can carry anything.
Deepgram native through the gatewayNeeds a Deepgram key and moves audio out of Cloudflare.
Keep Robots and poll fasterRobots cannot hear the take until Mux has an asset, which takes tens of seconds. It cannot reach the target.
One whole-take POST at Stop300 s of audio took 1.9 s plus a 9.6 MB upload, and long takes would fail outright.
Progressive title shown during or at Stop regardless of tailWrong subject on short takes (measured). A title that visibly swaps after Stop is worse than a placeholder.

Risks

  • Talking through the click may turn out to be common on short takes. The headline would then be about 1.2 s. The census RUM point measures it, and a socket for the open chunk is the measured fallback.
  • The energy gate could judge a quiet real word as silence and skip the tail. The eval PR measures false skips on the corpus.
  • The account limits are 720 speech and 300 text requests per minute. They are fine today but have a ceiling (about 120 to 240 concurrent recorders, inferred).
  • Discarded takes are now transcribed too. That is a privacy copy question, not a technical one.
  • What mip_opt_out means under Cloudflare's no-training statement is undocumented.
  • The mic-switch bug means a switched take loses audio entirely. It is reported as a follow-up and not fixed here.

PR sequence

  1. Merge #1397. Receipt: its eval and its 12-take artifact. In prod, Finish-to-title RUM sits around 58 s, which is the baseline.
  2. Live-speech eval, no runtime change. scripts/ai.mjs eval live-speech replays the corpus (kept outside Git) through the pure chunker, nova-3 and the title task. It reports word match and span overlap against Robots, cover math, energy-gate false skips, and confirms that a replayed chunk makes zero model calls. Gate: within the whole-take bands above.
  3. Server. Migration 0045 (chunk rows, per-take summary row, seal columns), the chunk route with the existing checks, cover, readTakeWords, and the speech GET built on it. Receipt: a script replays a corpus clip chunk by chunk against a PR preview, reads back complete words, and shows a replayed seq producing no ai_call point.
  4. Client. Feed reducer, PCM journal, seal on finish, Auto trim from live words, leave guard covering unacked speech, and the stop_silence_s census point. The same PR deletes the window search, /speech/window, /speech/recorder and recorder_speech_edges. Receipt: verify-loop on a preview covering Stop, a 60 s offline window, a killed tab that recovers with live words, and discard.
  5. Title at Stop. autoTitled with the live basis, the final chunk answering with the committed title, the editable title field, and stop_to_title_ms RUM. Receipt: p50 and p90 over 20 scripted preview takes, and #1397's title eval rerun on live words compared with Robots words.
  6. Instant path. Title drafts at pause cuts, committed by the finish intent when the seal matches. The long-take rule (take of 60 s or more and tail of 10 s or less) is included only if the eval keeps it inside full-vs-full noise. Receipt: stop_to_title_ms split by path, and the share of takes on the instant path.
  7. Robots as fallback for recordings. Skip mux_speech when the live cover is complete. Receipt: preview D1 shows no Robots job on live-complete takes and Robots words on a forced gap.
  8. Captions from live words (your call). Publish the live words as a Mux text track at asset ready and drop generated_subtitles. Then captions, transcript, title and speech bar are one set of words. Receipt: the share page plays captions built from live words.
  9. Uploads through the same chunks. Decode upload audio in the browser with WebCodecs and a demuxer while the file uploads, and post the same chunks, admitted for kind='upload'. Target (guess): title within about 2 s of the upload finishing for files under 30 minutes. Until then uploads keep Robots, with words about 20 to 60 s after asset ready. Receipt: words present at asset ready on a 10 minute upload.

What #1397 should do now

Merge it unchanged. Its ownership rules, follow rule, text task with gateway reach, title eval, video_title job and migration 0044 are all independent of the trigger, and the fast path needs every one of them. It gives every take a title in about a minute today, where there is none, and stays the backstop for takes the live path cannot cover. The fast path changes two things on top of it. autoTitled gains a live basis next to the asset basis, and mux_speech becomes conditional for recordings. Reworking it first would delay a working title by the whole stack and save no migration. All three design runners and the judge reached this independently.

Decisions for you

  1. Is about 1.2 s acceptable when the rep talks through the click? The alternative is a Durable Object streaming session at 1.77x per minute to save 0.3 to 0.5 s. The recommendation is to accept for now and let the census decide.
  2. Privacy copy. Whole takes, including discarded ones, now reach Workers AI while recording. Do you want an in-recorder notice, or only the privacy-page sentence?
  3. Captions. Keep Mux's auto-generated English captions, or replace them with a track built from the live words, which gives one transcript and supports nova's 10 languages?
  4. Editing the title on the finish screen makes it a person's title (title_source = 'user'). Yes?
  5. Raise the speech rate limit from 10 per minute per owner to 60 per minute per video?
  6. Merge #1397 now. Its migration rebuilds jobs, so it needs a night-window deploy.

Method

Three architect runners ran in parallel. Fable designed freely, one Opus seat argued the strongest case for chunked HTTP, and another Opus seat argued the strongest case for streaming. A Fable judge scored them on six criteria and picked the chunked-HTTP design as the base (25 points against 23 and 21). The parent then grafted parts of the other two onto it. The Fable synthesis seat hit its usage limit, so the parent wrote this page from the judge's verdict. Grounding came from two read-only code traces of main and #1397 and a docs survey. Probe scripts and raw results live in /tmp on the build machine and are not committed.
