Why public playback fails

Diagnosis for the 9.78% Mux playback failure rate, the 70% of player loads that never play, and the 31% of played sessions that freeze. Production read only. Window: 7 days to 2026-10-01 21:00Z.

The 9.78% is mostly robots. Four datacenter networks produced 13 views in the week. Every one of the 13 failed, with zero playing time. Microsoft's AS8075 alone is 10 of them. Take those 13 out and real viewers failed 9 times in 212 views, which is 4.25%, under Mux's 4.89% global average.

The 70% never-play figure measured our own hidden players, not prospects. Our loaded counter fires on the media element's loadeddata. Until preload="none" reached production on 30 Sept at 22:00Z, every hidden player downloaded video on page render and booked a load with no human involved. In the 23 hours after that deploy, 21 of 29 loads played. That is 72%, not 30%.

The 31% freeze figure is mostly slow startup counted as a freeze. 29 of the 54 frozen sessions report exactly one frozen second, and 33 of 54 report it before the second of playback. The sampler starts ticking on play and the clock has not moved yet.

One number did get worse, and it is real. Median startup went from 693 ms to 1,580 ms across the same deploy. We bought the byte saving with a second of waiting after the tap, and we are now slower than Mux's 1,169 ms global median.

Not the assets

Every one of the 14 Mux assets that carried a failure is status=ready with both a video track and an audio track and an empty error list. They were created between 2024-07-27 and 2026-09-30, and they include both the old direct uploads and the batch re-ingested by URL on 22 Sept. Failures do not pile onto any one asset: the busiest asset took 61 views and failed 3.3%, and every asset showing 100% failure has between 1 and 3 views, which is a crawler visiting once. The legacy-rendition theory is dead.

Why 70% of player loads never played

Our loaded row comes from a loadeddata listener on each element carrying data-video-id. loadeddata needs no user gesture. It fires as soon as the browser has a first frame, so before 30 Sept any player the page had rendered, visible or hidden, booked a load while it buffered. The fix that stopped hidden players downloading video also stopped the phantom loads. Splitting the same week at the deploy makes that plain.

Mux never believed the 70% either. Mux opens a view on a playback attempt, and it counted 178 views in the pre-fix window against our 404 loads. Only 3 views in the whole week ended with exit_before_video_start. Our counter was measuring something Mux does not call a view, and our dashboards read it as refusal to watch.

Two smaller contributors sit under the same number. Pages carry more than one video: of 583 sessions with a load, 127 loaded three videos and 3 loaded four, so one session can book four loads and one play. And 214 of the 407 sessions that loaded without playing never sent a page_unload beacon at all, which is what a client that does not run pagehide looks like.

One corroborating signal from page RUM over the same week. Of 594 desktop public page loads, 211 reported a largest-contentful-paint and 383 did not. Mobile reported one on 664 of 740. Desktop Safari and Firefox do not implement the LCP API, so some of that gap is browser support, but not two thirds of it.

Why 31% of played sessions looked frozen

The stall sampler reads currentTime once a second while the element is nominally playing and counts the seconds it did not move. It starts on the play event. Between the tap and the first frame the clock sits at zero while the player fetches its manifest and first segment, and the sampler books those seconds as a freeze.

Median startup is now 1,580 ms and the slower half of sessions sits well past two seconds, which is exactly long enough for the sampler to find the clock unmoved between its first and second tick. Strip the startup artifact and the real freeze rate is at most 21 of 176, around 12%, and Mux's independent rebuffer measurement of 1.12% suggests even that is generous. The long stalls are real and worth chasing, but they are a handful of sessions, not a third of them.

The post-fix window has 47 views and 2 failures, so its 4.26% carries a wide error bar. The startup move is the firmer of the two: 693 to 1,580 ms is more than a doubling, it is consistent with cutting 10 MB of speculative download, and it puts us behind the global median instead of ahead of it. A prospect on a phone now waits a second and a half after tapping play. Warming metadata for the first player only, or on poster press, would get most of the byte saving back without the wait.

Ranked causes

  1. Datacenter crawlers, counted as prospects (13 of 22 failures, likely 17) Four datacenter networks produced 13 views and 13 failures with zero playing time. Four more failures carry the Microsoft fleet's exact Chrome build strings with no resolvable ASN. We send Mux no metadata at all, so there is no dimension in Mux Data that separates a crawler from a buyer and this had to be reconstructed from ASN lookups. Fix: tag each view server-side from the request's network and user agent, and consider not booting the player for datacenter clients at all.
  2. A load counter that fires without a human (the whole 70%) loadeddata is not a play. The preload="none" change on 30 Sept already took the measured play rate from 17% to 72% without anyone touching the metric. The native-file path still ships preload="metadata" and still books loads with no gesture, so the counter stays wrong for file-backed videos. Fix: record play intent on the play event and stop inferring it.
  3. A stall sampler that counts startup as a freeze (33 of 54 frozen sessions) The sampler begins at play, when the clock is still at zero. Fix: do not count a frozen second until the element has advanced at least once in the session. Startup already has its own measurement in RUM and in Mux.
  4. Startup regressed to 1,580 ms (real, and ours) Median startup more than doubled across the preload deploy and is now above the Mux global median. This is the one number in the brief that got worse rather than being measured wrong.
  5. Instagram's Android in-app webview on slow carrier networks (5 of 22 failures) Every failure that belongs to a real viewer landed here, all of them error code 2, on carriers in Tunisia, India and Nepal. The webview is also our single biggest surface at 90 of 225 views, and it fails 5.6%. Modest, network-bound, and the only one of these causes that costs a customer a sale today.

Ruled out

  • Bad or legacy renditions. All 14 assets behind a failure are ready, complete and error-free, old uploads and 22 Sept re-ingests alike.
  • A player version. 221 of 225 views ran mux-player 3.13.4. The 4 views on other versions are too few to read.
  • A CDN or region. Fastly 1.2% on 86 views, edgemv 0% on 42. 21 of 22 failures never reported a CDN, so they failed before delivery.
  • Autoplay. 20 views with autoplay on, 0 failures.
  • A single bad page. The 22 failures spread across 11 pages, and the 6 pages Microsoft touched account for most of the concentration.

What I could not determine

  • Whether the 4 unresolved-ASN failures are the same Microsoft fleet. The build strings match exactly, but Mux returned no ASN for them and I have no second source to join on.
  • Per-view detail. /data/v1/views returns 404 for this account and real-time monitoring returns 402, so there is no view-level record to open. Everything here comes from filtered breakdowns.
  • Cloudflare bot scores for the public pages. The API token in this environment has no zone access, so I could not confirm the crawler share from Cloudflare's side.
  • Whether the two long freezes (179 s and 159 s) were network stalls or a player bug. One session each, no error reported, nothing else to join on.
  • Whether preload="none" suppresses loadeddata on mux-player by construction. I inferred it from the measured zero bytes before interaction and from loads matching plays after the deploy, rather than reading the player's source.
  • The post-fix failure rate within useful bounds. 47 views and 2 failures is not enough. It needs another week.
  • PostHog and Sentry corroboration. Neither credential is present in this environment.
