How other apps show an automatic edit

Research for Clarity's auto-trim status. Built from help pages and design guidelines fetched on 6 October 2026, not hands-on use. Anything marked guess is my inference with no source behind it.

Short answer

Apps that edit automatically almost never give the hand-edited state a control position of its own. They mark the machine's work, show what changed (a checkmark, a gray removed region, press-and-hold for the original), and offer two differently named exits, one to re-apply the automatic result and one to go back to the original. Once you touch the automatic result it becomes your edit, the auto mark goes away, and a way back stays in reach. No product I checked uses a half-on switch, and Apple's guidelines keep the mixed state for checkboxes that summarize sub-items. For Clarity, keep B's chip and drop the ✦ when the rep drags a handle. Put two verbs in the popover that name where they go, Use auto-trim and Use full video. Draw the cut silence on the timeline as gray regions the rep can restore.

Patterns

1. Auto as a toggle button

Seen in Lightroom mobile ("tap Auto again to turn it off")6 and iMovie's highlighted Auto button29.

Works when auto is one reversible step and nobody edits afterwards. Confuses after a hand edit, because neither page says whether the button is still on. Lightroom Classic shows what happens without it. Users asked Adobe for exactly this toggle8. I found no docs for "filled when applied, outlined once edited". guess It may exist in apps whose help pages don't describe button states.

2. Explicit mode picker: Auto, Manual, Original

Zoom low light defaults to Auto, and choosing Manual reveals a slider31. Apple lists "Original" as a peer in the filter row3, and Audio Mix offers "Standard: Play the original audio you recorded"5. A dropdown like Trim: Auto ▾ is the same pattern.

Works when the states are peers and switching costs nothing. Confuses when the manual value is set by direct manipulation somewhere else. Clarity's handles are that case, so the picker would have to jump to Custom on its own.

3. Modified marker plus a reset verb

Apple's blue checkmark on Mac1 and the outline ring on iPhone3, VS Code's colored bar with Reset Setting39, Final Cut's green checkmark27.

Works for many independent values, since it says "this one changed" without a mode. Confuses when "Reset" doesn't say where it goes. Lightroom mobile needs four reset destinations to stay clear7.

4. Separate verbs for the original and for auto

Apple pairs Auto with Revert to Original1,2, Riverside pairs Apply with Revert to original20, and Descript offers Discard all edits14.

Works whenever there are three states, which Clarity has. Confuses when both verbs are called Reset.

5. Hold to compare

Click and hold or the M key on Mac2, tap and hold in Google Photos10, press and hold in Lightroom mobile6.

Works for stills, where the difference shows in one frame. guess A trim's difference is time at the edges, which a held frame can't show. The gray cut regions in pattern 6 do that job for video.

6. Removed parts shown in place, restorable one by one

Riverside shows removed pauses gray with strikethrough and offers Restore21. Descript does the same with ignored text13. Audiate leaves a marker with Restore Audio32. Loom shows removed timeline parts in light gray19, and Screen Studio has Disable or Remove per zoom35.

Works when the removals are few and discrete. Clarity has exactly two. Confuses at scale, so Tella and Riverside add a bulk reset33,20.

7. On by default, off in settings

YouTube automatic chapters are checked for new uploads30, Screen Studio has Create zooms automatically36, iOS Auto-Brightness is on by default38, and Riverside has studio-level defaults23.

Works for a trusted default. The per-video control stays quiet and the global off switch lives in settings. Tella's count-and-confirm step33 suits opt-in tools, not defaults.

Google Photos' suggestion row10 may be an "applied chip" pattern, but the help page doesn't describe how a chosen suggestion looks, so I left it out.

The hand-adjusted state

This is the state D's half switch tried to show. The products split three ways.

The edit becomes the truth, with no label. After Lightroom's Auto the sliders just hold numbers8. YouTube's manual chapters override the automatic ones30. A revert verb stays available.

A "changed" marker, not a mode. Apple's checkmark and outline ring1,3 and VS Code's bar39 say a value differs and offer a way back. Descript colors AI edits blue14, which marks the machine's work rather than yours.

An explicit mode. Zoom makes you pick Manual before the slider appears31. Screen Studio sets auto or manual on each zoom35.

None of them uses a half-on switch. Switches are for "two mutually exclusive options" and "should take immediate effect" (NN/g)40. Apple's guidelines give the mixed state to checkboxes whose sub-items differ. They also say "Outside of a list, use a button that behaves like a toggle, not a switch"41. The deeper problem is that a switch has one off position, and Clarity has two states that aren't auto, the rep's trim and the full video. "Off" can't mean both.

Recommendations for Clarity

Two rules apply to every option. First, never use a bare "Reset". Name where it goes, as in Use auto-trim and Use full video. Second, the ✦ marks machine work only and disappears as soon as the rep drags a handle.

This is the simplest to build. Its gap is that removing a hand trim takes two steps (go back to auto, then pick full video) or dragging the handles out.

Avoid

Avoid the half-on switch. If a switch has to stay, make it two-state, let a drag turn it off with the line reading "Your trim", and give the full video its own link. Skip a confirm-before-trim step, which suits opt-in tools like Tella. A global Auto-trim new recordings switch in settings lets the per-video control stay quiet (pattern 7).
