## Where the time went

- faststart 164 s: full download of 889 MB, `-c copy` remux, full-decode verification of the output, SHA-256, 106 sequential 8 MiB part PUTs through the Worker, each hashed twice.
- clip 648 s: ~490 s CRF-20 libx264 encode of all 694 s (trim always re-encodes, `playback.py:153`; no input seek; 30 fps floor), plus download, full-decode verification, hash, sequential upload.
- poster: 3 s of work, 10 m 50 s of waiting. First attempt lost the lock; the 30 s re-alarm was cancelled because the recorder's client-captured poster PATCH made `ensurePosterForVideo` stand down; no cron covers posters; the pre-clip job row is orphaned as `failed_recoverable` with attempts 0.
- transcript 744 s: 653 s lock starvation (audio extraction is a third heavy operation on the same single-lock container) + 76 s extraction streaming the whole Original through the Worker + provider call.
- outbox 4 m 24 s: cron-only drain; never the fast path.

## Structural causes (from the three explorer reports in this directory)

1. One video = one container instance = one `PROCESSOR_LOCK` for the whole operation. All four kinds pass the same `videoId` to `containerInstanceName` (`workers/media-artifact-producer/src/index.ts:788`). Adding shards cannot help; contention is intra-video by construction.
2. The same busy reply is interpreted two ways: clip/faststart treat `status === 'processing'` as still rendering (renew lease, poll in 5 s); poster/gif/transcript treat it as a capacity failure and `deferForCapacity` (attempts refunded, 30 s alarm). Clip and faststart that hit `markFailed(recoverable)` are locked out for a fixed 600 s on the claim path while the advisory check says 120 s.
3. Terminal video state is a hand-written metadata field with one "ready" writer (`durable-faststart.ts:197`) that runs before the clip; the clip path never corrects it; the faststart path can never re-run.
4. Job identity moves: the poster digest hashes the served URL, so pre-clip and post-clip posters are different rows and the earlier one is orphaned.
5. Heavy recipes: trim = full re-encode; every output verified by full decode; sequential 8 MiB uploads through the Worker; the 889 MB Original is pulled from R2 three times across the four jobs; the transcript reads the fragmented Original forever.
6. The durable operations path emits no timing telemetry; only poster/gif/audio do.
7. Historical wall times (last 30 days, ready jobs): clip avg 13544 s (max 1.06 M s, stale outliers), faststart avg 365 s, transcript avg 20 s, poster avg 14 s.
