Why the editor has two editing surfaces

History synthesis, 2026-10-06, from three investigators over git history, Linear and the design site. Confidence tiers follow the pstack epistemics framework.

The question

Why does the Journey Page editor have two editing surfaces, the right-side panel and the on-page canvas, and why has removing the panel not moved since the 2026-09-08 design doc? Which recorded decisions and constraints already bind inline text, block reorder, add and remove, media, settings with nothing to click on the page, and edits by the built-in AI or a future MCP server? William wants to delete the panel one control at a time and make each editing operation consistent for people and agents.

The code in question

Paths are relative to /Users/williamlarsten/.t3/worktrees/clarity/t3code-7d957146.

  • The panel. The panel is the editor-panel aside and its editor-form at editor/src/routes/EditorWorkspace.tsx:239-330. It renders one Accordion per block with BlockEditor from block-editors.tsx. Each row has a grip driven by useBlockReorder, focus-revealed move buttons and a delete X. The panel also holds "+ Add section" and stores its open state under clarity-editor-panel-open.
  • The canvas. The canvas is LivePreview.tsx with EditableText, InlineText, InlineRichText, CtaRowEditor, CanvasReorder, CanvasAddSection and FaqListEditor. FaqListEditor also defines useListReorder.
  • The shared write path. Both write through use-editor-page.ts and journey-page-document-store.ts. Those call the domain package's BLOCK_REGISTRY, moveBlock, removeBlock and mergeDrafts.
  • The MCP registry. The registry at workers/frontend-router/src/agent/registry.ts:64 holds only whoami.

What we found

Why the editor has two editing surfaces

  • [Direct] The form panel and the editable preview arrived together in the commit that created the React page builder. Source: commit 96c2643c2, Willayam, 2026-04-09. It created EditorRoute.tsx, LivePreview.tsx, EditableText.tsx and FormFields.tsx for a builder with TipTap rich text and a live preview. Two days earlier, #18 aimed the React editor "closer to Angular behavior with richer form editing".

  • [Direct] In March the plan was a fully inline editor built on Puck. It was never built, and no record says why it was dropped. Source: docs/streaming-ai-editor-design.md in commit 536280a83, #12, 2026-03-22, chose "Inline editing via Puck's contentEditable". #24 closed unmerged on 2026-06-10, and #291 deleted the doc on 2026-06-18.

  • [Direct] The July block model kept the accordion as the structure editor. It postponed on-page manipulation without rejecting it. Source: WIL-3163, done 2026-07-07, says "The form column becomes a dynamic accordion list driven by blocks[]" with add, remove and up/down move. Its out-of-scope list calls "Drag-and-drop reordering and preview-canvas direct manipulation" "UX layering on the same model". User story 12 asks that "inline click-to-edit in the preview" keep working. Commit 96bea4d2f built this, with the preview "wired to per-block patches".

  • [Direct] The decision to keep two renderers per block kind is about the preview versus the published page, not about the panel. Source: WIL-3163 and ADR 0006 at 7f3cd4cf7^:docs/adr/0006-journey-page-block-document.md:9. Both rejected "One shared renderer for public page and editor preview" because "the preview is an interactive editing surface". The comment at editor/tests/block-rendering-parity.test.ts:27 keeps the rule.

  • [Direct] The stated reason to remove the panel is fewer controls and less to learn. Source: WIL-3195, created 2026-07-09 and now a trashed duplicate. It asks for "direct manipulation on the page itself (WYSIWYG) instead of control panels, so people can edit properly with less UI to learn", and says "knowing which editor controls are actually used should drive what gets cut". On WIL-3264, 2026-07-21, William wrote "I want every part to be wysiswyg and not have the side panel at all basically".

  • [Direct] The panel is still here because the chosen method keeps it, collapsible, until every control has another home. Source: the WIL-3277 comment of 2026-07-23 08:27. William rejected all three editor-wide grammars: hover chrome, click-to-select and a structure rail. The direction became to "chip the side panel away control-by-control", "with the panel collapsible in the meantime". The design doc mocked nine editor-wide idioms. It concluded "to decide one section at a time and reuse chrome the canvas already has", and "the panel stays until the last one" (7f3cd4cf7^:docs/design-docs/inline-editor.md:33, :43). #530 calls "when the panel dies" "the map's unspecified rollout question". WIL-3276 still lists the rollout as not yet specified.

  • [Direct] On 2026-09-04 a triage pass restated the direction. Controls sit beside the content, and page-wide actions go in a slim top bar. Source: the AI-generated triage comment on WIL-3276, headed "Product direction confirmed". It says "Editing controls appear beside the selected content on the page" and "Keep a slim top bar for page-wide actions such as theme, preview, share, undo, and publish." The same day #575 closed with "The current product direction is the single-page, direct-editing editor tracked by WIL-3276."

Text editing

  • [Direct] Visible text is edited where it is published, and the caret is the only affordance. Source: WIL-3277, 2026-07-27, list-grammar rule 1, "Every visible string edits where it is published; nothing the page can show goes in a card", and "the caret is the affordance, as in the CTA labels". William's decision that day reads "page-carries-the-text wins; the card mode is killed."

  • [Direct] The commit rules for inline labels are settled. Source: WIL-3277, 2026-07-26 15:05. A CTA label uses "contentEditable, commit on blur, Esc/Enter blurs, empty snaps back, no focus ring". Edit locks keep "at most one card ever open" as "pure derived state", with "no timers, no imperative blur".

  • [Direct] The recorded intent is one plain-text engine, and the fold has not happened. Source: the comment at InlineText.tsx:4-5 says EditableText is this component plus an align toolbar. It asks the next person to fold it in and not "grow another copy". The AI-generated triage comment of 2026-09-04 on WIL-3276 asks for "One text-editing engine" that owns "blur commit, Escape, paste cleanup, IME handling, length limits, placeholders, and an optional alignment toolbar". Commit 7d89ae087 on #575 did the fold, and #575 was never merged. The two engines still differ. InlineText snaps an empty value back (InlineText.tsx:50), and EditableText commits it.

  • [Direct] Rich text stays on one Tiptap configuration because React 19 breaks a React-owned contenteditable that holds HTML. Source: #536, 2026-07-28. React 19 "compares that prop by object identity", so each keystroke rewrote the HTML and killed the caret. Tiptap now owns the canvas field, "sharing one editor config with the sidebar field", so the HTML both produce "cannot drift". EditableText.tsx:19-21 repeats the constraint. docs/SECURITY.md:8 sends both public rendering and previews through sanitizeRichText.

  • [Direct] No outside write may remount a block while a rep types in it. Source: commit e2288ec38, 2026-08-13, found that "a contentEditable draft lives in the DOM, not in state". An autosave echo therefore made typed text vanish "with no blur to commit it". #530 found that a contentEditable moved in the DOM "loses focus without firing blur", which it called "silent data loss". #916 keeps the rule that "a server echo never remounts blocks mid-typing". For the same reason, beginPointerDrag blurs the focused element before every drag (editor/src/lib/reorder.ts:141-145).

  • [Direct] A known CTA blur hazard has no recorded fix. Source: WIL-3295, 2026-08-13. It says the blur cleanup sends a blank label to removal, which also blanks the primary CTA's link, and that "the fix belongs in the blur path". CtaRowEditor.tsx:258 still calls removeCta for a blank, unpinned label. For the primary CTA, removeCta writes an empty label, link and style (:129-133).

Block identity and reorder

  • [Direct] Block ids are durable because engagement analytics key on them, and every draft write refuses duplicates. Source: WIL-3163 says "section_id = block id, durable across rename and reorder". ADR 0006, CONTEXT.md:175 and the WIL-3276 notes repeat it. Commit 12e1341cc, in #968 for WIL-3514, merged 2026-10-02. It moved the duplicate check into serializeJourneyPageDocument because AI generation and recorder share pages skipped the route gate.

  • [Direct] Reorder is a drag with a keyboard equivalent. Per-row up/down buttons were rejected as a pointer control. Source: WIL-3277 rule 4, 2026-07-27, and #530. The buttons were "built and removed" because "the item slides out from under a stationary pointer, so a second click silently acted on its neighbour."

  • [Direct] Only the panel offers keyboard and screen-reader reorder. Source: #572, 2026-08-13, added arrow keys on a focused grip and focus-revealed "Move X up/down" buttons. Its reason was that "screen readers in browse mode consume arrow keys, so the arrow route alone would have been a regression". The canvas grip is aria-hidden with tabIndex={-1} (CanvasReorder.tsx:177-178). The design doc makes this step 4 and says the canvas grip "must take those over before the panel goes" (inline-editor.md:20).

  • [Direct] The editor hook enforces hero-first on purpose, so domain writers do not. Source: #572 says "The hero-first invariant stays an authority at moveBlockTo rather than moving into the domain's moveBlock, which would silently change behaviour for callers this brief fenced off." Today:

    • The domain's moveBlock and removeBlock have no hero rule (blocks.ts:706-726).
    • validateJourneyPageDocument checks only that ids exist and are unique (blocks.ts:820-843), and that is the only content check the draft write path runs besides Library references.
    • mergeDrafts puts the hero first (merge-drafts.ts:63).
    • The panel renders its delete X for every block, the hero included (EditorWorkspace.tsx:306-316).

  • [Direct] Reordering a list must renumber seq_no, because the public renderer sorts on it. Source: #530 and WIL-3277, 2026-07-28. FAQ reorder "never reached the published page" until questions.ts renumbered rows. The same note warns "pricing and form fields sort by compareSeqNo too (render-blocks.ts:576, :660) and will hit the identical two-part bug the moment they get reorder." The FAQ rule survives at questions.ts:5-15. The pricing and form warning lived only in QUALITY.md until #818 deleted it, and no comment on main carries it now. The design doc adds "Commit label edits keyed by the row id, never its index" (inline-editor.md:62).

  • [Direct] Canvas drops resolve by block id, and the panel's drag depends on all rows having the same height. Source: the comment at reorder.ts:52-57 says drops are "Resolved by id, never by rendered position", because blocks that render nothing are absent from the DOM. 24-block-reorder.css:1-4 calls its row fold "load-bearing". scripts/check-css-budget.mjs:49 keeps that file unlayered because layering "lets Accordion.css keep panels open during a drag, breaking the equal-row reorder maths". The CSS slice that covers it has sat on an unmerged branch since 2026-09-21 (commit 279fcc446).

  • [Direct] Each drag moves to the shared drag mechanism when its control moves to the canvas, not in a separate rewrite. Source: the WIL-3276 comment of 2026-09-04, "Port each interaction when its page control moves, not as a separate rewrite." Gallery, summary columns and media resize still bind their own pointer listeners (GalleryEditor.tsx:54-55, SummaryTableEditor.tsx:175-176, JourneyMediaResizer.tsx:72-73). WIL-3331 and #810, 2026-09-29, found that the pointer must be captured on the grid, not on the moving cell.

Add, remove and undo

  • [Direct] Hide/show is gone, and removing a section deletes it. Source: commit e20c377b7, 2026-07-27, "The hide toggle duplicated deletion in the header and confused more than it helped", and "adding sections is solely the add-block menu". WIL-3163 story 3 asked to "remove a block entirely rather than just hide it". That settled the hidden-sections question WIL-3277 raised on 2026-07-22.

  • [Direct] Delete works only from the panel, and add on the canvas works only with a mouse. Source: removeBlockById has one caller, the panel X at EditorWorkspace.tsx:311. #546 left keyboard and touch access to the canvas add trigger out of scope because the trigger is hover-only. It adds that "the sidebar bar covers those users". CanvasAddSection shows its insertion point only on page mousemove (AddSection.tsx:89).

  • [Direct] Adding a row and typing into it is one gesture. Source: WIL-3277, 2026-07-27. The FAQ ghost row "adds a question and takes the caret". The design doc reuses that ghost row for form fields.

  • [Direct] The editor has no undo, and the undo question was never answered. Source: WIL-3278 asks what undo "the panel-free editor" needs, because "Direct manipulation plus ~1s autosave with no history makes accidental edits permanent". It has no comments. A search of editor/src finds no undo implementation. The WIL-3276 direction of 2026-09-04 lists "undo" among the top-bar actions. The repo has a precedent in WIL-3184, "auto-trim auto-applies, with a visible undo", where "Suggest-only was rejected as friction."

AI, agent and MCP edits

  • [Direct] Every writer saves against a revision and merges field by field on conflict. Source:

    • Commit 9659a417d, 2026-10-01: "PUT /api/journey-pages/:id saves only over base_revision. A stale base answers 409 stale_revision with the current draft and its revision, and changes nothing."
    • Commit c162137cc merges "field by field, into nested objects; lists stay one unit."
    • #998, for WIL-3535, 2026-10-04, makes the revision required at compile time.
    • #874 says that when two writers change the same field or list, "the one saving second keeps its version of it."

  • [Direct] AI regeneration follows one document-level rule, with no per-block pinning. Source: WIL-3192, resolved 2026-07-20 with "William blessed". The automatic generation pass runs only while the document is machine-authored, and any human save turns it off for good. The editor chat lane's edit mode, which "preserves untouched blocks" with a human in the loop, is "the pinning mechanism". Per-block provenance is the deferred upgrade.

  • [Direct] The MCP door has one operation, and the next PR adds only reads. Source: registry.ts:64 holds [whoami], from #1012. Contradictions the plan has to resolve

  • [Direct] The record holds three different rules for tab order and keyboard reorder. Source:

    • WIL-3277 rule 5, 2026-07-27, forbids visibility: hidden because it "would drop the grip out of the tab order".
    • The next day the same ticket deleted "the dead grip keyboard path" in favour of Alt with the arrow keys on the question. #530 took the FAQ row buttons out of the tab order.
    • The design doc's form bar says "Hide it with visibility, so its controls leave the tab order" (inline-editor.md:60).
    • WIL-3277 rejects per-row up/down buttons as a pointer control. #572 keeps them as focus-revealed buttons for screen readers.

  • [Direct] The 2026-09-04 direction speaks of a selection, and the July verdict killed click-to-select. Source: the AI-generated WIL-3276 comment puts controls "beside the selected content". William's 2026-07-23 verdict rejected click-to-select. The record does not say whether "selected" means a selection model or only focus and hover.

  • [Direct] Newer artifacts calm the panel instead of removing it. Source: the roadmap line, the simplicity audit and William's 2026-10-06 request all say remove it. Against them:

    • The before-and-after page mocks "A calmer editor panel" with "Hidden until filled" marks "in the panel and on the canvas". That is a new hidden state, two months after hide/show was removed.
    • All four design directions, V2 to V5, keep a right-hand inspector. V5 rejects "Floating property popovers on the canvas", which is the idiom the design doc picked for Submit.
    • WIL-3540 tried V2 with "the familiar accordion editor". It closed as Won't fix on 2026-10-05 with no reason recorded.

  • [Direct] The agent designs disagree on whole-document writes versus block operations. Source: the 2026-09-30 design and the ownership audit want typed block commands. The 2026-10-02 build design rejects pages_edit_blocks. #304 kept a shared action layer as a reference only.

  • [Direct] The audits disagree on one renderer versus two. Source: the 2026-09-30 audit says on-page editing "makes the React canvas the product". It lists "One React block renderer for editor and public page" in what a rebuild from scratch would look like. The 2026-10-01 audit dismisses "Renderer unification", and the styling audit says "Do not merge the renderers".

  • [Direct] Registry-driven generation is both promised and dropped. Constraint set for removing the panel

Preserve

  • Durable block ids. Every add, move and delete keeps block ids, and serializeJourneyPageDocument refuses duplicates. Every new operation, for people and agents alike, should address blocks and list rows by id. Sources: WIL-3163, CONTEXT.md:175, 12e1341cc.

  • One hero, always first. Today only moveBlockTo, mergeDrafts and generation hold this rule (#572, merge-drafts.ts:63).

  • The typing rules.

    • Commit any edit in progress before the DOM moves.
    • Never remount a block on an identical server echo.
    • Key commits by row id, never by index.

    Sources: e2288ec38, #530, #916, reorder.ts:141-145, inline-editor.md:62.

  • Rich text. Keep one Tiptap configuration, and keep sanitizeRichText on both renderers (#536, docs/SECURITY.md:8).

  • List order. Every list that reorders renumbers seq_no and keeps identity in an id, as questions.ts does for FAQ (#530).

  • The save protocol. Every write carries a revision, a stale write gets a 409 with the current draft, and the merge goes field by field with lists as one unit (9659a417d, c162137cc, #998).

  • Two renderers. The parity test holds them together, edit mode keeps the published skeleton, and the SSR renderer does not change (ADR 0006, block-rendering-parity.test.ts:27, WIL-3276 out of scope).

  • The chrome rule. Nothing is drawn at rest, chrome stays minimal and edge-mounted, and pressable controls are ButtonBase with no theme tokens (WIL-3311, #572, styling audit).

  • The settled list grammar.

    • Text edits where it is published, and the caret is the affordance.
    • Edit locks keep at most one card open.
    • Row controls sit in the row's own layout.
    • A ghost row adds an item and takes the caret.
    • The hover card holds settings with no pixel.

    Sources: WIL-3277 of 2026-07-26 and 2026-07-27, and the design doc.

  • Keyboard and screen-reader reach at least equal to the panel's today. That means grip arrows, focus-revealed move buttons and live-region announcements, plus keyboard paths for add and delete (#572, #546, inline-editor.md:20).

  • Delete, the top bar, desktop-first. Delete means delete, with no hide toggle. Theme and other page-wide actions stay in a slim top bar, and editing stays desktop-first (e20c377b7, WIL-3277 of 2026-07-23, WIL-3276).

  • The agent write rules.

    • Record, replay and revocation exist before any write.
    • Page documents returned to agents are marked untrusted.
    • Impersonated sessions cannot write.
    • Edits to a video that a live page plays need approval.

    Sources: WIL-3544, #1024, the build design.

  • The regeneration rule. Each document carries one machine-authored bit. Edit mode preserves untouched blocks, and generation never invents media (WIL-3192).

Risk

  • Lost typing. A write from the AI, an agent or a second tab that replaces block objects can remount a contentEditable and drop text the rep has not committed (e2288ec38, #916).
  • List conflicts. Lists merge as one unit. An agent's edit to one FAQ row and a rep's edit to another row in the same block collide, and the second saver's list wins (merge-drafts.ts:76-78, #874).
  • Delete versus edit. The outcome depends on save order, and the rule written in WIL-3477 does not match the code. Canvas and agent deletes inherit this (merge-drafts.ts:23-26, merge-drafts.test.ts:109).
  • Hero deletion. The panel can delete the hero today, and nothing on the server refuses a page without one. A canvas delete or agent write that copies the panel's behavior carries this over (EditorWorkspace.tsx:306-316, blocks.ts:721-726, :820-843).
  • No-op reorder. A pricing or form reorder that only moves array items will look right in the editor and change nothing on the live page (#530, render-blocks.ts:576, :660).
  • Accessibility. Keyboard, screen-reader and touch users lose reorder, delete and add if the panel goes before the canvas covers them (CanvasReorder.tsx:177-178, AddSection.tsx:89, #546).
  • The CTA blur path. It still clears the primary link when the label is emptied. A shared text engine could copy that pattern into every field (WIL-3295, CtaRowEditor.tsx:129-133, :258).
  • A panel coming back. The calmer-panel mock and the inspectors in V2 to V5 could bring a panel back after this work deletes one, since no direction has been chosen.
  • Large agent writes. Whole-document agent writes are large and carry untrusted text. pages_get with the full document may exceed Claude Code's 25,000-token result cap (build design, #1024).
  • Telemetry. Deleting the panel deletes its telemetry before anyone has read it (#658, EditorWorkspace.tsx:294-311).
