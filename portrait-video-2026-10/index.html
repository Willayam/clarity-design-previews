Portrait video in Clarity

Research for William, 2 October 2026. The report comes first. The sketches below it compare the card and viewer treatments it refers to.

The short version

The blue is painted into the video file, so no screen can undo it. Fix the recorder on phones first. Record the camera itself, with no canvas, and the public page already shows a tall portrait frame. Then give the Library card a blurred backdrop taken from the clip itself (sketch A below). Multi-track recording gives flexibility only on desktop, needs a renderer we deleted in September, and is not where the performance win is. Defer it until a rep asks to move the camera after recording.

Two models wrote this independently (Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra) from the same four research briefs. They agreed on every main call. Where they split, the pick and the reason are noted.

1. What is happening

The recorder records a 1920×1080 canvas, not your camera (editor/src/hooks/use-canvas-compositor.ts:9). Every frame starts with a radial blue gradient (:80). The camera is then fitted inside without cropping (:107). A phone held upright sends a portrait feed, so each frame is a strip of you about a third of the width, on blue, and that is what gets encoded (editor/src/hooks/use-media-recorder.ts:147). Mux measures the upload as 16:9 and the videos row stores that. The gradient came with the 2025 Angular recorder and nobody wrote down why. The live monitor plays the canvas too (editor/src/routes/RecordVideoRoute.tsx:703), so you see the strip while recording.

Every surface shows the strip because every surface is honest about a dishonest file. The public page already sizes its frame from the ratio Mux measured (workers/frontend-router/src/public-video-policy.ts:93, packages/journey-page-domain/src/journey-page-styles.ts:277). #913 shipped that and #955 backfilled it. About 945 production takes are not 16:9. A real 9:16 file renders as a tall frame on a phone, capped at 75svh, and a test covers it. The recording is the only thing in the way.

It also breaks the design rule we wrote down. DESIGN.md:10 says the rep's video is the object and Clarity's chrome recedes. DESIGN.md:12 rules out gratuitous gradients. This one is painted inside the rep's content.

2. Portrait, surface by surface

Recorder on a phone. Record the camera track itself. No canvas. iOS Safari has no screen share, so one MediaRecorder on the camera stream is the whole job. The asset becomes a true 9:16 and the per-frame canvas work disappears. Gate it on "this browser cannot share a screen", not on sniffing for a phone. Show the monitor in the camera's own shape. There is a nearby bug. Three of the four layout tiles do nothing on an iPhone, because the missing screen share fails silently (editor/src/hooks/use-screen-share.ts:33). Hide them there.

Recorder on desktop. Keep the compositor for now. Webcams are 16:9 and fill the canvas, and switching layout mid-take is the point of the picker (editor/src/components/RecorderLayoutPicker.tsx:86). A raw track cannot switch layout. Replace the gradient with a flat dark mat. WIL-3409 and WIL-3416 would then save black clips, not blue. Their real fix is refusing to start without a video track.

Library card. Sketch A, own-frame blur. The grid keeps one rhythm, the sides are the clip itself blurred, and 16:9 cards look exactly as today. The card must read the ratio the row already stores. Today it never does (editor/src/components/MediaCard.tsx:152 is a fixed 16:9 box). It should ask Mux for the still with fit_mode=preserve instead of smartcrop (workers/workspace-tree/src/workspace-tree-card-read-model.ts:13). One still, drawn twice. B shows the face bigger but crops 30 percent of the frame and breaks the rhythm. C crops screen recordings badly and hides the share button.

Inline player and edit modal. The inline player defaults to cover (editor/src/components/VideoPlayer.tsx:72) in a 16:9 box (:349). An honest portrait file would crop to the nose. Pass contain when the ratio is not 16:9, with the same blurred still behind it. The edit modal already uses contain, so leave it.

Filmstrip. Each tile is drawn into a fixed 112×63 canvas (editor/src/lib/video-filmstrip.ts:402), which squashes a portrait frame sideways. Draw with cover instead. The helper exists (drawVideoCover, :178). Astra preferred contain on a neutral tile. Cover wins here because the strip is for finding a moment, and squashed slivers are harder to read.

Public page on a phone. Already built. Nothing to do once the file is honest.

Public page on a laptop. D1, already built. Tall player centred, capped at min(56vh, 640px). D2 makes the rep smaller than D1 and adds decoration. D3 looks best and breaks the single-column rule. That is your call, in section 5.

Share image. Keep the 1200×630 smartcrop (workers/frontend-router/src/public-page-delivery/render-blocks.ts:730). Link previews are wide everywhere, and a face crop fills them. Astra wanted the full portrait on a neutral backdrop. That rebuilds the narrow strip we are removing, so Fable's pick stands. Check one real portrait take.

Email GIF. Nothing to build. Mux renders the animated GIF in the asset's own shape, up to 640px a side. Today it ships the gradient.

Old recordings. Takes already recorded keep their baked blue. Don't auto-crop them. Guessing the strip's edges from pixels will be wrong often enough to hurt.

3. Multi-track

Does it make performance better? Only partly, and the part that helps is not multi-track. A phone has one video source. Dropping the canvas removes a two-megapixel gradient fill, a draw and a frame capture per frame, plus the AudioContext clock. That needs no second track. On desktop with a screen, separate tracks remove the draw loop but add a second encoder and more upload bytes. Keeping an instant composite for the share link adds a third. WIL-3181's strongest argument was background-tab throttling, and the audio clock from May 2026 already handles that. The editor has no telemetry for dropped frames, fps or CPU, so nobody can show a desktop performance problem today.

Does it clean things up? Raw camera recording on phones deletes code. Multi-track adds it. The journal, upload grant, video row, Mux clip and edit manifest all assume one source (packages/video-derivations/src/manifest.ts:8). Separate tracks need a layout model, a sync manifest, two upload identities and a compositor somewhere.

Does it give flexibility after recording? On desktop, yes, and only there. A phone has nothing to re-layout. Every re-layout needs a renderer. The one we had, media-artifact-producer, went with #769. WIL-3189, WIL-3190 and WIL-3194 were closed the next day with a note to re-file against Mux.

A link that exists at Stop is not the same as a video that plays. None of these options changes the second number. WIL-3196 measures it.

WIL-3183 should stand. One baked MP4 as the only public asset is why the page, still, GIF, download and trim all work from one id. Only d breaks it, and d also has the iOS audio problem. 4. Recommendation and sequence

Build b now. Defer c and e until a rep asks to move the camera after recording, or telemetry shows the desktop composite dropping frames.

  1. Device day. Run the tests in section 6 and post the numbers to a ticket.
  2. Raw camera recording when the browser cannot share a screen. Monitor in the camera's shape, flat mat instead of the gradient on desktop. Done when a phone take on staging reports 9:16 in Mux and the public page shows the tall frame, with record-to-share time unchanged (WIL-3196).
  3. Library card A, plus contain in the inline player. Done when one portrait take sits beside a screen recording in the Library and both look right, light and dark.
  4. Filmstrip cover draw. Done when the trim modal shows an unsquashed portrait strip.
  5. Check og:image and GIF on one portrait take. Fix only if the crop misses the face.
  6. Recorder telemetry. Draw-loop fps and canvas-track fps per take, measured on the weakest laptop we can find. This decides whether c is ever worth building.

This touches WIL-3409, WIL-3416 and WIL-3414 in the recorder, WIL-3494 for the typed row the card reads, WIL-3196 for the metric that must hold, and WIL-3183, which stands. WIL-3189, WIL-3190 and WIL-3194 stay closed until step 6 or a customer gives a reason. Then re-file them against Mux as c plus e.

5. Calls only you can make

  • Card shape. A keeps the grid, B shows the face bigger and breaks the rhythm, C is a photo grid. Both models pick A.
  • Laptop viewer. D1 now, for free, or D3 as a hero layout that breaks the single-column rule (DESIGN.md:123). Both models pick D1 now and D3 as a later experiment.
  • What sits behind a desktop screen composite. A flat dark mat, or a colour the rep chooses, the way Vidyard does it. I pick the mat.

6. Facts that need a device test before building

  • Does Mux honour Safari's MP4 rotation flag? Safari stores a portrait recording as rotated metadata, not rotated pixels (WebKit bug 198912). Upload one raw iPhone take to staging. The asset must say 9:16 and the still must be upright. If not, the fallback is a canvas at the camera's own size with no gradient.
  • What the iPhone negotiates. Width, height and fps after the first frame, front and back camera, Safari and Chrome. It reports 1920×1080 for the first few hundred milliseconds before flipping (livekit issue 2099). Also whether rotating mid-take changes the track.
  • Whether the streaming upload and record-to-share time hold on a raw track.
  • Whether smartcrop finds the face in a true portrait still at 400×250 and 1200×630.
  • Later, for c. How many 1080p MediaRecorders a weak Windows laptop runs without dropping frames.

Sources
